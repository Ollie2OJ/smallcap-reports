Industry context

Koppers operates in wood preservation chemicals, treated wood products (railroad ties, utility poles), and carbon materials/chemicals (creosote, carbon pitch, naphthalene) derived from coal tar. Structural industry demand for treated wood in railroads and utilities is described by industry researchers as stable-to-modestly growing, with the global fungicide wood treatment market projected to expand at "a compound annual growth rate of 3.5-4.5% from 2026 through 2035, driven primarily by replacement demand in electrical utility pole and railway cross-tie infrastructure." However, this growth coexists with structural headwinds specific to Koppers' legacy carbon/creosote chemistry: reports note "the EPA's revised creosote risk assessment is decoupling traditional wood tie demand from volume growth," EU regulation is "phasing out creosote, pushing operators toward copper-based and organic alternatives," and utility operators are increasingly substituting composite and steel poles for wood. Feedstock supply for Koppers' carbon materials business is also constrained: coal tar availability has declined as North American steel manufacturers reduce output, a factor management itself cited in closing Stickney. Tariffs are also flagged as a market-wide cost pressure, with one market report noting tariffs "increasing costs of imported chemical compounds, copper-based ingredients, biocides, and formulation additives." The competitive landscape in wood treatment is consolidated, dominated by a handful of integrated players.

What Accruals ratio measures

Measures. How much of reported profit is NOT backed by cash: net income minus operating cash flow, divided by total assets.

What would make you look closer. A high positive number means profit is not turning into cash — worth asking which working-capital line explains the gap. Roughly above 0.10 is the level at which most readers would go looking.

What it means for the business.
